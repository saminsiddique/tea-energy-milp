YES — H₂ available
β Decision — Optimizer chooses based on COE minimization
β = 1 · H₂ Sale
When: Dry season — high solar & wind, abundant H₂, good market price.

Action: Surplus hydrogen is sold to local market (fertilizer production priority).

Power backup: Biomass generator handles remaining demand.
Fuel Cell is NOT activated here.

COE effect: Revenue reduces total cost.

Biomass Generation
Activated on the LEFT side in two cases:
① β = 1 path: Biomass is the only backup (FC is OFF, H₂ is being sold).
② H_h ≤ H_min: Not enough H₂, so biomass is the fallback.
P_h^BM = Q_h^B · η_st Q_h^B = B_h · LHV · (1 − e_Ls) LHV: 17.5 MJ/kg (dry) → 14.3 MJ/kg (wet)

H₂ for Electricity — Fuel Cell (FC)
Stored hydrogen is fed into the PEM Fuel Cell to generate electricity and fill the demand gap. This is the primary backup on the deficit side.

ε-Constraint MILP Optimizer — For Min COE vs Max Reliability
All Re-check paths feed back here. The optimizer adjusts component sizing and ε bound, then re-runs the entire dispatch loop. The Pareto front is built by varying ε across many iterations.
[optimization feedback] → HRES Output {PV, WT} → Load Balance → … → Optimum Output Values
Key correction from previous version: The RIGHT side (Demand Deficit / S₂) does not contain a β decision at all. The paper's Fig. 4 explicitly shows a "No H₂ Sales" box on the right side — meaning β is irrelevant there. β only lives on the LEFT side (Surplus / S₁), after hydrogen has been produced. The FC being "stopped" when β = 1 is correct behaviour: on the surplus side, biomass replaces the FC as backup when hydrogen is being sold.
